The hard questions

Intelligence has to
survive execution.

A dark factory needs a connected account of the world, the actions available, and the evidence that an action accomplished its purpose.

01

A goal has to become a verifiable outcome.

What physical state must change? Which tolerances, process conditions and acceptance criteria determine whether the work is complete? The system needs this contract before it can choose a plan.

02

A plan has to respect the actual machine.

A tool call is only useful when it resolves to a machine capability, a valid operating state and an execution interface. Resource availability, interlocks, timing and control authority are part of planning.

03

Adaptation has to include recovery.

Industrial execution encounters variation, wear, missing material and unexpected state. I’m interested in systems that detect divergence, identify what remains achievable and recover without losing track of the original outcome.

04

Learning has to carry over to the next task.

The long-term question is how experience from one machine, process or site reduces the work required at the next. Representations must retain the physical information that transfers and expose the constraints that do not.

05

Verification has to close the loop.

The system should connect its claimed result to independent observations of the physical work. Completion, confidence and missing evidence need explicit meanings.
